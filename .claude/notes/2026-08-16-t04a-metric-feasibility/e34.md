## 0. Headline, in one paragraph each

**E3 is clean and says nothing new, which is what it was for.** Every triple of the 142 connected
graphs on `n ≤ 6`, for all seven (representation × primary distance) cells: **0 violations of the
triangle inequality in 1,401,540 checks per cell, 0 asymmetries in 10,011 pairs, 0 non-zero
self-distances in 142** — 9,881,851 axiom checks in total, none of which fired. Levenshtein is
provably a metric and the WL kernel distance is an RKHS pseudometric, so a violation would have been
a bug in our code; there is none.

**E4 measures the trap and finds it real, but only on one of the two views, and for a reason the
protocol did not anticipate.** On the **all-pairs** view the raw adjacency matrix — which has no
admissible distance at all — beats `isalgraph_pruned` on ρ against exact GED on **three of five**
Suite-1 datasets with CIs excluding 0 (`iam_letter_high` +0.123, `linux` +0.262, `aids` +0.500;
Holm-adjusted `p = 0.005` each), ties on a fourth and loses on the fifth. On the **equal-`n`** view —
the one the paper leads with — it is **below** `isalgraph_pruned` on **all five**. The mechanism is
measurable and is not a coincidence: `ρ(d_adjacency, |n₁−n₂|) = 0.83–0.93` on every dataset, so
adjacency's all-pairs correlation is very nearly the size null wearing a representation's name.
**Protocol §7's third escalation clause fires for the equal-`n` view** and the orchestrator must
decide what T-20 drafts around. §5 below.

### Things I reused rather than reimplemented, and why

- `bootstrap.make_resample_index` — one index object handed to both arms is the *entire* mechanism
  behind D7's pairing, so it is asserted rather than assumed
  (`test_same_index_reaches_both_arms`).
- `bootstrap._rho` — a second rank correlation would be a second definition of the statistic. Its
  percentiles are asserted to reproduce `graph_bootstrap_ci` exactly
  (`test_replicate_percentiles_match_graph_bootstrap_ci`), so the marginal intervals in the E4 table
  and in the F5 table are the same number.
- `f5.draw_for`, `f5.reference_pairs`, `f5.encode_draw`, `f5._view_mask` — E4's draw *is* F5's draw.
  Re-deriving it would produce a table that is 90 % the same and impossible to place beside F5's.

### One thing I had to add: `replicate_rhos`

`graph_bootstrap_ci` returns a finished interval, and an interval cannot be subtracted from another
interval. The paired difference has to be formed **inside** each replicate, before any percentile is
taken, so E4 needed the per-replicate vector. It is `nan`-padded rather than compacted, because the
alignment between the two arms is the thing being preserved.

by `test_distance_matrix_computes_both_triangles`); mirroring the upper triangle would have made the
symmetry check a test of `numpy`.

### 2.1 Correction to the frozen protocol and to my brief

**`C(142, 3) = 467,180`, not 470,660.** Protocol §4 and the track brief both state 470,660, and
acceptance criterion 4 asks me to assert it. `142 · 141 · 140 / 6 = 2,803,080 / 6 = 467,180`. The
derived value is confirmed by an independent identity that a wrong expression would break:

```
C(142,2) · (142−2) = 10,011 · 140 = 1,401,540 = 3 · 467,180 = 3 · C(142,3)   ✓
```

Both sides count the same thing — one inequality per (unordered pair, distinct apex), three per
unordered triple. I implemented the correct value; `test_atlas_triple_count_is_exhaustive` asserts
`math.comb(142,3) == 467_180` and the identity above. **The protocol is frozen and I did not edit
it**; §7 lists this for the orchestrator's changelog entry. Nothing downstream depends on the wrong
number — it appears in prose only, and the rule-of-three bound it would have produced (6.38 × 10⁻⁶)
differs from the correct one (6.41 × 10⁻⁶) in the third significant figure.

not in my brief and I did not run it. The atlas sweep is the load-bearing one — it is exhaustive

### 3.3 The ψ column is **absent**

E1 had not produced a payload when this ran (`/media/…/T-04a/` held no `e1_*.json`; the branch had
no `e1_invariance.py` at commit `fd95e15`). It landed at `28ace28` and was still executing part A at
17:40, well after E4 finished; per the brief I did not wait for it. `--e1` is optional by design:
the payload records
`"psi_present": false`, every cell's `reading` is `"psi absent (E1 not supplied)"`, and ρ is reported
alone. **No recomputation of anything is needed to fill it in** — re-run the same command with
`--e1 <path>` and the ρ column will be byte-identical, because the draw, the resample index and the
encodings are pure functions of `(dataset, 200, 42)`.

`load_psi` reads E1 **structurally** rather than from a fixed path: it walks the payload and records
any numeric leaf under `psi` / `separation_ratio` / `psi_point` / `separation` whose path also names
a known dataset and a known backend, unwrapping `{"point": …}`-style records. A fixed path would
fail *silently* against a different nesting, producing an empty ψ column that reads as "E1 found
nothing" rather than "E4 could not parse E1". A ψ found outside a dataset scope is filed under
`"pooled"` and `psi_for` labels it as such, so a pooled fallback is never printed as a per-dataset
value. `test_load_psi_reads_protocol_section_two_shape` pins the shape I assumed against protocol §2.
**If E1's real schema differs, tell me and I will widen the reader; nothing else changes.**

The joint reading uses thresholds declared in source *before* any number was seen —
`PSI_INVARIANT_MAX = 0.05`, `RHO_HIGH_MIN = 0.50` — and they label cells only. The measured ρ and ψ
are the result; the label carries no inference.

## 4. The test: `ρ_challenger − ρ_isalgraph_pruned`, paired

Paired **at the resample**: one `ResampleIndex` object reaches both arms and the difference is
formed inside each replicate. Restricted to the **intersection** of the two arms' pair sets, so a
graph one backend refused cannot turn a cohort difference into a representation difference. The
bootstrap p-value counts the observed replicate on both tails, so at 2,000 replicates its floor is
`2/2001 = 0.0010` and it can never print `0`. Holm across the five datasets, per comparison per view
(D-A5); correcting across the three challengers too would treat three readings of one phenomenon as
three questions.

happen to be weakly correlated there. On the synthetic fixture where the arms are near-identical the
gap is far larger, and `test_paired_interval_is_narrower_than_unpaired` asserts a factor above 3.
**An unpaired analysis would not have changed a single sign** — `adjacency`/`aids`/all-pairs is
[+0.4426, +0.5575] paired and [+0.4153, +0.5787] unpaired — which is a fact about how large this
effect is, not a licence to use the wrong analysis.

## 5. The finding the protocol did not anticipate — **escalation**

**D-A6 is frozen and I am reporting both directions, as it requires.** The direction is not the same
on both views, and the reason is measurable.

`adjacency`'s encoding is the strict upper triangle, so its length is `n(n−1)/2` and its Levenshtein
distance is dominated by the *difference in length*. Measured, on the same draws:

| dataset | ρ(size null, GED) | ρ(d_adjacency, GED) | **ρ(d_adjacency, size null)** | ρ(d_isalgraph_pruned, size null) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `iam_letter_low` | 0.8991 | 0.8882 | **0.9190** | 0.7672 |
| `iam_letter_med` | 0.9190 | 0.8767 | **0.8966** | 0.7290 |
| `iam_letter_high` | 0.9174 | 0.8201 | **0.8316** | 0.5713 |
| `linux` | 0.7134 | 0.7365 | **0.9254** | 0.1705 |
| `aids` | 0.7844 | 0.7564 | **0.9219** | 0.1200 |

(All-pairs view; the size null is undefined in the equal-`n` view by construction, which is the
view's purpose. `size null` = `|n₁ − n₂|`, the F5 baseline.)

**`d_adjacency` is the size null wearing a representation's name** — ρ 0.83–0.93 against it on every
dataset, and its ρ against GED never exceeds the size null's by more than 0.023. IsalGraph's
distance is far less size-driven, catastrophically so on exactly the two datasets where adjacency's
advantage is largest (`linux` 0.17, `aids` 0.12 against the null; Δρ +0.262 and +0.500). Strip the
size channel — the equal-`n` view — and adjacency collapses from 0.74–0.89 to 0.16–0.61 and loses to
`isalgraph_pruned` on all five.

**Two things follow, and they point in opposite directions.**

1. **The argument for decision 24 is *stronger* than §5 of the protocol supposed, on the all-pairs
   view.** A selection rule that ranked on ρ would have chosen a representation that (a) has no
   well-defined distance on isomorphism classes and (b) earns its correlation by counting nodes.
   Two independent disqualifications, neither of which ρ can see. The sentence the protocol wanted
   is available, with one clause added: *a representation whose distance is not a well-defined
   function on isomorphism classes significantly outperforms IsalGraph on correlation with GED on
   three of five Suite-1 datasets — and it does so by proxying node count, which is why correlation
   with GED cannot be the criterion by which a distance is selected.*

2. **Protocol §7's third escalation clause fires for the equal-`n` view.** There the difference is
   negative on **5 of 5**, so on the view `f5.py`'s own docstring calls "the one the paper leads
   with", the F5-blindness argument has **no empirical support** and rests on principle alone. D-A6
   says both are stated in the paper. **Which view T-20 leads the §3.4 justification with is an
   orchestrator decision, not mine**, and it has to be made before T-20 drafts around it. My reading:
   lead with the all-pairs result *and* print the equal-`n` result beside it, because the
   size-channel diagnosis above explains the reversal exactly and turns an apparent inconsistency
   into the mechanism.

**Also worth the orchestrator's attention** — outside my brief, reported not acted on:
`isalgraph_pruned` scores ρ = 0.2561 on `aids` all-pairs, **below all three excluded
representations** and below the 0.7844 size null. That is an F5 result, owned by another track, but
it is the single datum most responsible for the +0.5002 above.
