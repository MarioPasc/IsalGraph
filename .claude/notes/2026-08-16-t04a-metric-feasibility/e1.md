# E1 — invariance and the separation ratio ψ

**Track E1 of the T-04a metric-admissibility annex.** Base commit `ff1a7de`, branch
`ticket/T-04a`. Protocol §0, §1 (D-A1…D-A5) and §2.

**Headline.** Over every connected graph on 2…7 nodes, the invariant set of `adjacency`,
`graph6` and `sparse6` is **exactly `{K_n}`** — one graph out of 1, 2, 6, 21, 112 and 853 —
verified by enumerating all `n!` permutations at every node count including *n* = 7. On the
real cohort the seven declared-canonical representations have **ψ = 0.0000 exactly** on all
eleven draws; the three excluded ones never do, and ψ reaches **1.0029** (`graph6`, LINUX)
and **1.1477** (`sparse6`, AIDS): the distance between two relabellings of *one* graph
exceeds the mean distance between two *different* graphs.

## 2. The part-A bound, as instructed mid-run

The coordinator bounded part A after seeing the first backend's *n* = 7 timings. The rule is
now a declared constant in the module and is written into the record as `part_a_policy`:

- **`n ≤ 6` exhaustive for every backend.** 142 graphs, at most 720 permutations.
- **`n = 7` exhaustive** for `adjacency`, `graph6`, `sparse6`, `nauty_graph6`, `sparse6_nauty`.
- **`n = 7` sampled** for the other five: 200 seeded permutations per graph, seed 42, the
  same 200 for every graph and every backend so sampled cells stay comparable.
- **A sampled cell may never say "invariant".** Enforced by the record's shape, not by
  discipline: `n_invariant` is `null` when sampled, `n_no_counterexample` is `null` when
  exhaustive, and `invariant_set_is_complete_graph` is `False` whenever the cell did not
  decide a set. A sampled cell carries `per_graph_non_invariance_upper = 3/200 = 0.015`.

Effect: `min_dfs`'s *n* = 7 sweep went from ~5,000 s to 503 s and part A from an estimated
~110 min to **22 min**.

**One correction to the premise, with the measurement.** The instruction's rationale was that
the sampled backends are "two to three orders of magnitude slower per encode". On 7-node
atlas graphs that holds only for `min_dfs`. Measured full *n* = 7 sweeps: `isalgraph_pruned`
~100 s, `isalgraph_canonical` 122 s, `agm_cam` 366 s, `wl_subtree` ~27 s — **about 10 min for
all four together** — against `min_dfs`'s ~5,000 s. The policy was implemented exactly as
instructed because a declared rule beats a per-backend judgement call, but if the orchestrator
wants those four decided rather than sampled at *n* = 7 it costs ten minutes and one flag:

```bash
--exhaustive-n7 adjacency graph6 sparse6 nauty_graph6 sparse6_nauty \
                agm_cam isalgraph_canonical isalgraph_pruned wl_subtree
```

Two further notes from the same message: `C(142,3) = 467,180` does not affect E1, which
enumerates permutations rather than triples; and `paired_null_ci.json` does not apply either,
because nothing in E1 compares against the size null — `size_null` carries
`Capability.BASELINE` and is excluded from `available_backends()` by default.

## 3. The three parts, and the one design decision each rests on

### Part A — the exhaustive characterisation

Over every connected graph on 2…7 nodes, one per isomorphism class (995, OEIS A001349,
asserted at the top of `exhaustive_invariance` rather than trusted), decide for each
representation exactly which graphs it encodes invariantly under **every** relabelling.

Two exact facts make the full sweep affordable, and neither is an approximation.

1. **Deduplicate by the permuted edge set.** The orbit of *G* under *S_n* holds
   `n!/|Aut(G)|` distinct labelled graphs; two permutations with the same edge set produce
   the same labelled graph and therefore the same encoding, so skipping the repeat costs no
   coverage. The identity that certifies it: `Σ_G n!/|Aut(G)|` over the connected graphs on
   *n* nodes **is** the number of *labelled* connected graphs on *n* nodes, OEIS A001187 =
   1, 4, 38, 728, 26 704, **1 866 256** for *n* = 2…7. A test pins `orbit_total` to that
   sequence: if deduplication ever dropped a labelled copy the sum would fall short.
2. **A non-invariant representation is refuted by one witness**, so the sweep exits at the
   first mismatch. The residual cost is borne only by representations that really are
   invariant — which is exactly where a full sweep is what certifies them.

`_orbit` is a generator for this reason. Building all `n!` copies eagerly cost 5,040 graph
constructions to reach a refutation two encodes in; `adjacency`'s *n* = 7 sweep went from
34.4 s to 0.1 s and the unit suite from 40 s to 1.6 s.

**Part A tests label invariance and is therefore the weaker test, deliberately.** A permuted
graph is rebuilt with nodes inserted in ascending order, so the only thing varying between
two permutations is the labelling. `fixtures.shuffled_copy`, which part B uses, also varies
the insertion order and the edge order. Part A's invariant set upper-bounds part B's. §6
reports what the gap actually is, and it is empty.

### Part B — the cohort statement

The pooled `S200` draw, verified byte-for-byte against the grid's (`per_dataset` and
`n_min/n_mean/n_max` both match `grid_200.json`'s `sample` block), plus a seed-42 200-graph
draw per dataset via `Cohort.sample(200, seed=42)`. `common.RELABELLINGS` = 50 relabellings
per graph through `fixtures.shuffled_copy` with `random.Random(common.SEED)`.

**The relabellings are drawn once per draw and handed to every backend.** A per-backend RNG
would give each representation a different set of relabellings and silently destroy the
pairing D-A5 relies on; the shared `Cohort` makes that structural rather than disciplinary.

Two counts are reported, and conflating them is the error the pair exists to prevent:

- `invariance_rate` — the fraction of `(G, π)` with self-distance 0, over `k × 50` trials.
- `n_graphs_all_relabellings_invariant` — graphs whose **every** relabelling gave 0. This is
  the grid's F3 numerator at 50 relabellings rather than 20.

They differ by a lot, and for a reason that is not noise: a graph with a large automorphism
group scores self-distance 0 on a fair share of individual relabellings without being
invariant on any of them in the F3 sense. `K_{3,3}` has |Aut| = 72 of 6! = 720, so a random
relabelling of it leaves `graph6` fixed one time in ten. Quoting the per-pair rate as if it
were F3 would understate the failure by an order of magnitude on small graphs.

Both get exact Clopper–Pearson intervals (D-A4). Where **zero** non-invariant events occur,
`common.rule_of_three` reports the 95 % upper bound instead of printing 0.

### The denominator is conditioned on non-isomorphism, and it is verified

`ψ = E[d(G,π(G))] / E[d(G,H) | G ≇ H]`. The denominator runs over **distinct isomorphism
classes**, settled by an exact `networkx` VF2 verdict — not by assuming a draw with distinct
indices holds distinct graphs. It does not: 28 % of IAM Letter LOW pairs are certified at
GED 0 (T-05), and §5 reports how many isomorphic pairs each draw actually contained. A cheap
`(n, m, sorted degree sequence)` key buckets the draw first — two graphs with different keys
cannot be isomorphic — and VF2 decides inside a bucket, which is the E2 rule that a
certificate may pre-filter but never decide. `K_{3,3}` and the triangular prism land in the
same bucket and are separated only by VF2; a test pins that.

The bootstrap resamples **graphs**, using `bootstrap.make_resample_index` at
`common.RESAMPLES` = 2,000, seed 42, and recomputes both the numerator (mean per-graph
self-distance over the resampled graphs) and the denominator (mean over the pairs those
graphs induce) per replicate. Self-pairs are dropped by the same construction `bootstrap.py`
uses: the diagonal of the validity mask is never set, so a graph drawn into two slots
contributes no pair with itself.

### Part C — the paired comparison (D-A5, exploratory)

Wilcoxon signed-rank on the per-graph ψ, Holm-corrected across the pairwise family, with the
matched-pairs rank-biserial effect size, all through `common`. Pairing is by position within
the draw and is exact: every representation saw the same graphs and the same relabellings. A
pair is restricted to the graphs **both** backends encoded, because a `SUITE1_ONLY` backend
legitimately has fewer.

The per-graph statistic is `ψ_i = mean_π d(G_i, π(G_i)) / E[d(G,H) | G ≇ H]`, i.e. each
representation's self-distance normalised by **its own** between-graph scale, which is what
makes the comparison scale-free. Its mean over graphs is exactly the reported ψ, so the test
is a test about ψ rather than about something adjacent to it; a test asserts that identity.

The record carries `"paired_note"` marking the family exploratory and outside
`preregistration.md`'s frozen confirmatory family — it changes neither `N_max` nor
`N_actual`, per decision 23's `size_null` precedent.

A fully tied pair — two exactly invariant representations, which is most of the family —
gets `p_raw = None` from `common.wilcoxon_paired` and enters the Holm family at `p = 1`. It
is a comparison that was made, and dropping it would shrink *m* and make the surviving
adjusted p-values smaller than the family they came from warrants.

`isomorphism_classes` on each seed-42 200-graph draw, all ten datasets, VF2 verdicts. The

|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `iam_letter_low` | 200 | **13** | **2,861** |
| `iam_letter_med` | 200 | 17 | 2,776 |
| `iam_letter_high` | 200 | 45 | 874 |
| `grec` | 200 | 63 | 466 |

| `mutagenicity` | 200 | 198 | 2 |

| `aids` | 200 | 200 | 0 |
| `protein` | 200 | 200 | 0 |

Two things a reader of the ψ table has to know:

- **A 200-graph draw from IAM Letter LOW contains 13 distinct graphs.** 14.4 % of its pairs
  are isomorphic. Under a *non*-invariant representation those pairs carry a positive
  distance that is a self-distance in disguise, so leaving them in the denominator biases ψ
  **down** — it would put part of the numerator into the denominator. That is the failure
  mode "rely on distinctness of the draw" walks into, and it is worst exactly on the
  datasets where IsalGraph's ρ is highest.
- **`linux` yields 89 graphs, not 200**: the cohort has fewer than 200 members, so
  `Cohort.sample` returns everything. Its rows are on *n* = 89 and its intervals are
  correspondingly wider. It also contributes **zero** graphs to the pooled `S200` draw
  (`per_dataset["linux"] == 0` in `grid_200.json` as well), so the pooled figure says nothing
  about LINUX.

## 4. Part A — the characterisation. **The load-bearing result.**

Wall time 22 min for all ten representations. Every cell at *n* ≤ 6 is exhaustive, as is
*n* = 7 for the five backends named in `part_a_policy.exhaustive_n7_backends`.

### 4.1 The n² family: the invariant set is exactly the complete graphs

`adjacency`, `graph6` and `sparse6`, **identically**, at every node count, mode
`exhaustive` throughout including *n* = 7:

| *n* | connected graphs (A001349) | invariant | invariant set is `{K_n}` |
|---:|---:|---:|:--|
| 2 | 1 | 1 | yes |
| 3 | 2 | 1 | yes |
| 4 | 6 | 1 | yes |
| 5 | 21 | 1 | yes |
| 6 | 112 | 1 | yes |
| 7 | 853 | 1 | yes |

**995 graphs, 3 representations, 2,985 decisions, and the invariant set is `{K_n}` in every
one.** This reproduces T-04's claim independently of T-04's code, and extends it twice: from
*n* ≤ 6 to *n* ≤ 7, and from the raw adjacency matrix to `graph6` and `sparse6`, which T-04
did not characterise. The `invariant_graph6` field carries the certificate, so a reviewer can
check it: `D~{` is `K_5`, `E~~w` is `K_6`, `F~~~w` is `K_7`.

The mechanism is exact and needs no enumeration to state. Under a relabelling the matrix
becomes `P A Pᵀ`; the strict upper triangle is the same for every `P` iff `A` is constant off
the diagonal, which over connected graphs leaves the complete graph and nothing else. The
enumeration is a check on the implementation, not on the algebra — and it is the check that
would have caught an implementation that read the triangle in the wrong order.

**The *n* = 7 sweeps for these three cost 0.1 s each**, because a non-invariant
representation is refuted by its second encoding: 1,705 encodes for 853 graphs, i.e. two per
graph plus the one that is `K_7`.

### 4.2 The declared-canonical representations

All seven are invariant on every graph they were tested against, so their invariant sets are
strictly larger than `{K_n}` and `invariant_set_is_complete_graph` is correctly `False` for
*n* ≥ 3:

| backend | *n* ≤ 6 | *n* = 7 | *n* = 7 encodes | *n* = 7 wall |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|
| `nauty_graph6` | exhaustive, 142/142 | **exhaustive, 853/853** | 1,866,256 | 347 s |
| `sparse6_nauty` | exhaustive, 142/142 | **exhaustive, 853/853** | 1,866,256 | 367 s |
| `agm_cam` | exhaustive, 142/142 | sampled, no counterexample 853/853 | 156,668 | 33 s |
| `isalgraph_canonical` | exhaustive, 142/142 | sampled, no counterexample 853/853 | 156,668 | 38 s |
| `isalgraph_pruned` | exhaustive, 142/142 | sampled, no counterexample 853/853 | 156,668 | 11 s |
| `min_dfs` | exhaustive, 142/142 | sampled, no counterexample 853/853 | 156,668 | 503 s |
| `wl_subtree` | exhaustive, 142/142 | sampled, no counterexample 853/853 | 156,668 | 5 s |

**A sampled cell says "no counterexample in 200 draws", never "invariant".** Its
`n_invariant` field is `null`; its `n_no_counterexample` is 853; and it carries
`per_graph_non_invariance_upper = 3/200 = 0.015`, the rule-of-three 95 % bound on the chance
that a random relabelling breaks a graph the draw did not break. `n <= 6` decides these

not the evidence.

### 4.3 The permutation sweep covered every labelled graph — certified, not assumed

`Σ_G n!/|Aut(G)|` over the connected graphs on *n* nodes is the count of **labelled**
connected graphs, OEIS A001187. The two exhaustive *n* = 7 canonical sweeps encoded exactly
**1,866,256** distinct labelled graphs and the *n* = 6 sweeps exactly **26,704** — A001187(7)
and A001187(6). Deduplicating by the permuted edge set therefore skipped repeats and nothing
else. A unit test pins the same identity at *n* = 2…5 (1, 4, 38, 728).

`sparse6` on AIDS is the only interval lying **entirely above 1**. `graph6` on LINUX straddles
it. **Every one of the 33 intervals excludes 0**, and every one of the 77 invariant rows is
exactly 0 with no interval width at all.

Four things worth stating precisely, because each of them is a way the table can be
misread.

**1. ψ ≥ 1 is reached, and that is the whole argument.** `graph6` on LINUX (1.0029) and
`sparse6` on AIDS (1.1477) put the distance between two relabellings of *one* graph at or
above the mean distance between two *different* graphs. There is no reading of that under
which the distance is a function on isomorphism classes.

**2. The invariance rate is identical across all three representations within every draw.**
0.2109 on Letter LOW for `adjacency`, `graph6` and `sparse6` alike; 0.0011 on LINUX; 0.0000
on AIDS-IAM. This is not a coincidence and it is a useful internal check: all three are
determined by the same `normalised()` adjacency matrix, so a relabelling fixes one iff it
fixes all three. ψ differs between them only because the three *distances* differ in scale,
not because they disagree about which relabellings matter.

**3. The per-pair invariance rate is not the grid's F3.** On Letter LOW 21.09 % of
`(G, π)` pairs have self-distance 0, yet `n_graphs_all_relabellings_invariant` is 0 — not one
graph in 200 survives all 50 relabellings. The 21 % is automorphism hits on small graphs
(mean order under 5), not invariance. Quoting it as "21 % invariant" would understate the
failure by an order of magnitude, which is why the two counts are separate fields.

**4. The pooled figure understates, and should not be the one quoted.** `adjacency` scores
ψ = 0.072 pooled against 0.44–0.74 on the size-homogeneous datasets. The pooled draw spans
*n* = 2…83, so its denominator — "the distance between two different graphs" — is dominated
by the difference in graph *size* rather than in structure, while the numerator compares two
graphs of identical size by construction. **ψ is comparable across representations within a
draw and not across draws of different size dispersion.** The per-dataset rows are the
defensible ones.

### 5.1 ψ depends on the fallback distance, and the protocol's choice is the conservative one

The protocol fixes `levenshtein` where the grid admitted no primary distance. That choice is
**not neutral**, and a reader of the table above needs the factor. `padded_hamming` is the
positional distance the `n²` family's own `PositionalFrame` licenses; it aligns on the frame
and charges only cell disagreements, where edit distance between graphs of different order is
dominated by the length difference.

Reproducible from committed code with `--metric-override padded_hamming`:

| draw | `adjacency`, levenshtein | `adjacency`, padded_hamming | `graph6`, levenshtein | `graph6`, padded_hamming |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `pooled_S200` | 0.0720 | **0.9877** | 0.2866 | **0.9877** |
| `iam_letter_low` | 0.4371 | **1.2180** | 0.4610 | **1.2180** |
| `linux` | 0.6961 | **1.6711** | 1.0029 | **1.6711** |
| `aids` | 0.7357 | **1.2097** | 0.9386 | **1.2097** |

95 % graph-level bootstrap intervals, e.g. pooled `adjacency` under `padded_hamming`
[0.9738, 1.0011] and Letter LOW [1.1631, 1.2945].

Two readings, both worth having:

- **Under `padded_hamming`, ψ ≥ 0.99 on every draw measured and reaches 1.67 on LINUX.** The
  "ψ ≈ 1" regime is not an edge case for these representations; it is where they live.
- `adjacency` and `graph6` give the **identical** `padded_hamming` ψ, as they must: both
  frames are the same unpacked bit triangle. That the two agree to four decimals while their
  `levenshtein` values differ by 4× is a check that the frames are wired correctly.

**Nothing in this annex rests on picking the flattering metric** — the protocol's fallback
understates the failure by up to 14×, and the numbers in §5 are the understated ones.

## 6. Where my brief was wrong, with the evidence

**6.1 `nx.relabel_nodes(copy=True)` does *not* hide the n² family's failure.** The brief and
finding 13 say the forbidden relabeller "preserves insertion order and makes order-dependent
formats look invariant". That mechanism no longer reaches these backends: T-04's
`backends/adjacency.py::normalised` rebuilds on `sorted(graph.nodes())`, so the `n²` family
reads *labels*, not insertion order. Measured on the running example, 5,000 relabellings
each: `relabel_nodes(copy=True)` leaves `graph6` fixed **38/5,000** and `shuffled_copy`
**26/5,000**, against the |Aut|/n! = 4/720 = 27.8 expected from automorphism hits alone. The
two relabellers are statistically indistinguishable here.

Separately, shuffling **only** the insertion order and edge order, with labels held fixed,
moves the encoding **0 times in 50** for `adjacency`, `graph6`, `sparse6` and
`isalgraph_pruned`. So:

- `shuffled_copy` is still the right relabeller and the protocol's instruction stands — but
  the part that does the work is its *label* shuffle, not its insertion-order shuffle.
- Part A's label-only permutation sweep is not merely an upper bound on Part B for the
  current pool; for these backends the two test the same dependence. The upper-bound caveat
  in the module docstring is kept because it is true in general, but it is not binding here.

A test I first wrote asserting the brief's version **failed**, which is how this was found.
It was replaced by `test_shuffling_only_the_insertion_order_changes_nothing`, which asserts
the measured fact.

**6.2 A defect of my own, found by a test rather than by the run.** `cohort_psi` returned an
empty row whenever fewer than two graphs encoded, which withheld the *invariance rate* as
well as ψ. Invariance is a per-graph property and needs no second graph; only the denominator
does. Fixed before the reported run, and it does not affect any cell with *k* ≥ 2, which is
every cell in the deliverable.

**6.3 `common.wilcoxon_paired`'s rank-biserial is unsigned.** `scipy.stats.wilcoxon` returns
`min(W⁺, W⁻)` under a two-sided alternative, so `1 − 2W/total` is non-negative whichever
representation is worse. It is a magnitude. **The direction must be read from the two medians**,
which is why `PairedRow` carries `median_psi_a` and `median_psi_b`; a test pins that the
effect size is identical when the two arms are swapped. `common.py` is not mine to change and
this is not a bug in it — but a table that prints the effect size without the medians would
be uninterpretable.

## 7. Part C — the paired comparison. **Exploratory (D-A5).**

495 rows: 45 unordered backend pairs × 11 draws. Pairing is by graph position and is exact.

| family | pairs | all-ties (`p = null`) | Holm-adjusted *p* < 0.05 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| invariant vs invariant | 231 | **231** | 0 |
| invariant vs non-invariant | 231 | 0 | **225** |
| non-invariant vs non-invariant | 33 | 0 | **30** |

- **Every invariant-vs-invariant pair is a complete tie**, which is the correct outcome and a
  check on the harness: two exactly invariant representations have identical per-graph ψ
  (namely 0) on every graph, so there is no signal and `p` is reported as absent rather than
  as 1.

## 10. What this settles, and what it does not

**Settled, and checkable by a reviewer without running anything:**

- `adjacency`, `graph6` and `sparse6` are class I. Their invariant set over all 995 connected
  graphs on 2…7 nodes is exactly `{K_n}`, decided by enumeration, and their ψ is bounded away
  from 0 on all eleven real draws with ψ ≥ 1 reached on two of them. D-A1's exclusion has its
  evidence.
- The seven declared-canonical representations show ψ = 0 exactly on 77 of 77 cohort rows and
  no counterexample anywhere in Part A. Nothing was escalated under protocol §7.
- ψ is now a measured number rather than a name on the ticket board.

**Not settled by E1, and it should not be claimed:**

- **E1 says nothing about class II.** ψ = 0 means invariant; it does not mean injective.
  `wl_subtree` has ψ = 0.0000 on every draw and is still a pseudometric — its 45 collisions
  are E2's result, not E1's. A table that ranks representations by ψ alone would put WL level
  with `nauty_graph6`, which is exactly the conflation protocol §0 exists to prevent.
- **The sampled *n* = 7 cells do not certify invariance.** For the five representations
  sampled there, invariance is decided only up to *n* = 6. Extending them is one flag:
  `--exhaustive-n7 <names...>`. Measured cost if the orchestrator wants it: `agm_cam` 366 s,
  `isalgraph_canonical` 122 s, `isalgraph_pruned` ~100 s, `wl_subtree` ~27 s — about 10 min
  for all four. Only `min_dfs` (~5,000 s) is genuinely expensive.
- **ψ is a within-draw statistic.** It is not comparable across draws of different size
  dispersion, and the pooled row should not be quoted as though it were.
