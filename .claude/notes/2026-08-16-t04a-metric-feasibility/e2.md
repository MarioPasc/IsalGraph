`ticket/T-04a`. Protocol §0, §1 (D-A2, D-A4) and §3.

## 2. Part A — proof by exhibition

`K₃,₃` versus the triangular prism (`fixtures.K33`, `fixtures.PRISM`). Asserted, not assumed:
both have **6 nodes, 9 edges, degree set {3}**, both connected, and `nx.is_isomorphic` returns
`False`. 1-WL leaves the colouring constant after round 1 on two regular graphs of the same degree
and order, so refinement never starts and no number of rounds fixes it.

| representation | distance | separates |
|---|---|---|
| `wl_subtree` (kernel) | **0.0** | **no** |
| `nauty_graph6` | 3.0 | yes |
| `agm_cam` | 3.0 | yes |
| `isalgraph_pruned` | 5.0 | yes |
| `isalgraph_canonical` | 5.0 | yes |
| `min_dfs` | 5.0 | yes |
| `sparse6_nauty` | 5.0 | yes |
| `sparse6` | 6.0 | yes |
| `adjacency` | 9.0 | yes |
| `graph6` | 3.0 | yes |

One fixture settles the qualitative claim: **`wl_subtree` is a pseudometric on isomorphism classes
as a matter of proof, not of sampling.** No collision rate of zero on any cohort can overturn it,
and `classify()` enforces exactly that — an exhibited collision assigns class II even when the
sampled rate is zero (`test_an_exhibited_collision_makes_a_representation_class_two`).

**Read the last three rows carefully.** `adjacency`, `graph6` and `sparse6` separate the witness
too, and that is not evidence in their favour: class I *over*-discriminates, so it separates
relabellings of one graph as readily as two different graphs. Part A speaks only to the injectivity
direction. Their class comes from the grid's F3 and is not recomputed here.

### 3.2 Pooled rates

Rate **among zero-distance pairs** — the precision of the implication *`d = 0` ⇒ `G ≅ H`*, which is
the quantity the AE.3 uniqueness axis is about:

| representation | zero pairs | collisions | rate, 95 % |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| `isalgraph_pruned` | 7,038 | 0 | **≤ 4.26 × 10⁻⁴** (rule of three) |
| `min_dfs` | 7,038 | 0 | ≤ 4.26 × 10⁻⁴ |
| `nauty_graph6` | 7,038 | 0 | ≤ 4.26 × 10⁻⁴ |
| `sparse6_nauty` | 7,038 | 0 | ≤ 4.26 × 10⁻⁴ |
| `isalgraph_canonical` | 6,905 | 0 | ≤ 4.34 × 10⁻⁴ |
| `agm_cam` | 6,905 | 0 | ≤ 4.34 × 10⁻⁴ |
| `wl_subtree` | 7,083 | **45** | **0.00635 [0.00464, 0.00849]** |

Rate **among all evaluated pairs**: `wl_subtree` 45/183,016 = 2.46 × 10⁻⁴ [1.79, 3.29] × 10⁻⁴;
every complete invariant 0/180,321 or 0/108,290, i.e. ≤ 1.66 × 10⁻⁵ and ≤ 2.77 × 10⁻⁵.

**Recommendation for T-17's AE.3 table: quote the among-zero rate.** The among-pairs rate is
governed by how many duplicate graphs a cohort happens to contain, not by the representation — it
would report `wl_subtree` as more accurate on `aids` (0.00055) than on `grec` (0.00101) when the
truth is the reverse (100 % vs 4 %). Both are in the JSON; each carries its Clopper–Pearson interval
and, where the count is zero, the rule-of-three bound instead of a bare `0`.

**Interval caveat, carried in the JSON rather than left to a reader**: pairs within a dataset share
graphs, so they are not independent trials and the binomial interval understates uncertainty. D-A4
fixes Clopper–Pearson for proportions, so that is what is reported; the per-dataset rates are the
comparable ones.

### 3.3 The sharpest form of the finding

On the two cohorts with **no duplicate graphs at all** — `linux` (89 classes in 89 graphs) and
`aids` (200 in 200) — `wl_subtree`'s zero set is 1/1 and 11/11 collisions. **Every distance zero
WL emits on LINUX and AIDS is a false certificate of isomorphism.** Those are exactly the two
datasets on which D-A2 records that `wl_subtree` beats IsalGraph on ρ. That is the honest
side-by-side AE.3 asked for: WL correlates better with GED there *and* cannot tell two different
graphs apart there. It is retained and declared, per D-A2 — not excluded — and the scope limit
D-A2 names is exactly what these numbers make enforceable.

### 3.4 Coverage — the denominators are per representation, not global

| representation | evaluated pairs | encode refusals | why |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| `nauty_graph6`, `sparse6_nauty`, `wl_subtree` | 183,016 | 0 | full coverage |
| `isalgraph_pruned` | 180,321 | 14 `CanonicalizationTimeoutError` | 2 s budget |
| `min_dfs` | 180,321 | 14 `MinDfsBudgetExceeded` | 50,000-projection cap |

## 4. The converse direction — raised, never reported

For every representation, every VF2-certified isomorphic pair was checked to have `d = 0`.
**7,038 pairs checked per full-coverage representation, 0 violations, on every one.** Had one
fired, `converse_check` raises `common.AdmissibilityError` and the run stops with no JSON — an
invariant representation separating two isomorphic graphs is a defect in our code, not a property
of the method (protocol §7). `test_converse_check_raises_rather_than_reporting` is acceptance
criterion 5 and pins the raise.

For the six complete invariants, `zero_pairs == converse_pairs_checked` **exactly**. The zero set
and the VF2-certified isomorphic set coincide: over this cohort the induced distance is a metric on
isomorphism classes, not merely a pseudometric that happened not to collide.

### Where the isomorphic-pair set comes from, and what that makes conditional

As the brief instructed, the isomorphic-pair set is read off a canonical backend's own zero set —
the `nauty_graph6` encoding partitions each draw, and each class is then **certified by VF2 against
its representative**. So:

- **Soundness is VF2's.** A same-class verdict is transitive over two VF2 runs; a different-class
  verdict is a direct VF2 run. `pynauty` never decides anything. `test_the_oracle_rejects_a_lying_certificate`
  feeds the oracle a certificate that groups `K₃,₃` with the prism and asserts it still answers
  "not isomorphic", recording a `certificate_defect`. Zero defects were recorded on real data.
- **Completeness is conditional.** "These are *all* the isomorphic pairs" holds only if the nauty
  canonical form is a complete invariant. Part A and E1 establish that independently; the JSON
  states the conditionality in the `certifier.note` field of every dataset record rather than
  leaving it implicit.

## 6. Four things in the brief that were wrong, with evidence

1. **`linux` does not hold 200 graphs, so it is not 19,900 pairs.** The brief and protocol §3 say
   "all ten datasets, all `C(200,2) = 19,900` pairs". `len(datasets.load("linux").graphs) == 89`,
   so its seed-42 draw is 89 graphs and **3,916** pairs. Total pairs for a full-coverage
   representation are **183,016**, not 199,000. `Cohort.sample` already clamps with
   `min(k, len(graphs))`, so nothing broke — but a per-dataset denominator of 19,900 written into
   the paper would be wrong for one row in ten.

2. **The pair denominator is per representation, not global.** §3.4: `agm_cam` and
   `isalgraph_canonical` are `SUITE1_ONLY` and refuse 605 graphs each; `isalgraph_pruned` and
   `min_dfs` refuse 14. Their rates rest on 108k and 180k pairs respectively, and quoting one
   denominator across the table would overstate the evidence behind the two `SUITE1_ONLY` rows by
   40 %.

3. **The VF2 cost trap did not bite at this scale, and the honest number should be reported rather
   than the mitigation.** Total VF2 time across all ten datasets: **0.05 s over 752 calls**, slowest
   single call 3.66 ms (`mutagenicity`). Running VF2 on all 19,900 pairs per dataset really would
   have been ~199,000 calls, but running it on only the zero pairs — without any certificate
   partition — would have been ~7,083 distinct calls and roughly 1.5 s. The certificate partition
   is worth keeping because it is what supplies the converse direction's pair set, not because the
   VF2 cost was ever the binding constraint.

4. **A `pynauty` pre-filter in the sense the brief describes is not usable at all.** A pre-filter
   that skips VF2 when certificates *differ* is a certificate verdict of "non-isomorphic", and a
   pre-filter that skips VF2 when they *agree* is a certificate verdict of "isomorphic". Either way
   the certificate decides. The construction used instead — group by certificate, certify each
   class by VF2 against its representative, VF2 directly across classes — is the only shape in which
   the certificate reduces work without ever being the verdict.

Two smaller notes:

- **The zero tolerance never binds.** `ZERO_TOL = 1e-12` exists for float noise, and the count of
  pairs in `(0, ZERO_TOL]` is **0** across every (representation, dataset) cell — recorded as
  `near_zero_nonzero`, so the claim "both admissible distances are exact" is measured rather than
  asserted. Levenshtein is integer-valued and the WL kernel is a sum of products of small integers.
- **No bare `0` is printed anywhere.** Every rate record carries a `reported` string; zero counts
  render as `0/N; <= x at 95 % (rule of three)`.
