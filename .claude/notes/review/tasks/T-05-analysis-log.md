### Design decisions worth knowing

**The bootstrap is an algebraic reformulation, not a sampler.** For a graph resample with
multiplicity vector `c`, the sum of any symmetric matrix `F` over the induced pairs is
`S_F = ½(cᵀFc − Σ_a c_a² F_aa)` and the induced pair count is `(N² − Σc_a²)/2`. The OLS slope needs
only `S_y, S_x, S_xx, S_xy` and the count, so a replicate costs five quadratic forms. Batching 250
replicates into one GEMM turns 2,000 replicates over Letter HIGH's 2.1 M pairs into **under a
second**. It is an identity, not an approximation, and
`test_weighted_replicate_sums_equal_explicit_enumeration` plus
`test_bootstrap_slope_matches_a_naive_per_replicate_fit` assert it against explicit enumeration of
the induced pairs to `rtol=1e-9`.

**Why this matters beyond speed**: the whole eight-dataset analysis runs in **5.9 s**. That removes
the temptation to cut replicates, and it means the ten-dataset re-run is not a scheduling problem.

**The resampling unit and the self-pair rule are the repo's existing ones**, taken from
`ged_bakeoff_analysis.replicate_selection` / `induced_pairs`: `SeedSequence([seed, replicate])`, and
slots holding the same original graph induce a self-pair which is **dropped**. Two tests assert
equality with those functions rather than trusting that I reimplemented them the same way.

**Tier 3 is a separate code path** (`_bootstrap_slopes_subsampled`), since the frozen rule demands a
subsample the fast path would not take. It draws slot pairs uniformly without replacement via
`rng.choice(T, K, replace=False, shuffle=False)` and unranks them with
`ged_pair_index.pairs_from_indices_searchsorted`. Measured on a synthetic N=4,040: 48 ms for the
draw and 112 ms for the unranking, so ~0.4 s per replicate → **~7 min per dataset per arm** for the
two tier-3 datasets. Neither exists locally, so this path is covered only by unit tests, including
one that sets the budget to the whole population and asserts it reproduces the exact path.

## 4. Where I believe the brief is wrong

### 4.1 `seconds_matrix` is in-worker solver time, not pool-inflated wall time

The brief says the §7.4 numbers are "**realised wall time under a known-pathological
parallelisation**" and must never be read as per-pair cost. The caveat is right; **the direction is
backwards**. Three measurements:

1. Letter HIGH `lb` sums to **0.2859 ms/pair**. Amendment 11's production figure for that same
   campaign is **19.8 ms/pair** — **69× apart**. A table of realised wall time would have
   reproduced 19.8.
2. The rates sit at **1.67×** (Protein) and **3.07×** (Letter HIGH) of amendment 11's single-worker
   rates of 7,366 and 93 µs/pair. That is a contention slowdown, which is what a timer *inside* a
   worker records.
3. Amendment 7's Protein anchors — 14.0 ms/pair `lb`, 163.7 `ubs` — agree with my 12.289 and
   145.726 to within 12 %. Amendment 7 was reading this same quantity.

So the table **under-reports** the resource the campaigns consumed, by ~2 orders of magnitude on the
small-graph sets. The pool pathology lives almost entirely in the gap between these numbers and the
job wall clock. What survives inside the table is the residual contention factor, which is **unequal
across datasets** (1.67× vs 3.07×) and **not recoverable**, because no file's metadata records the
worker count. The cross-dataset comparison is still void — for that reason instead of the stated one.
The report carries the corrected caveat with all three checks. Fixed in `30e324f`.

**Consequence for amendment 11**: its withdrawal of amendment 7's conclusions was justified on the
grounds that amendment 7's figures were "measured inside this regime". They were — but at a 1.7–3×
contention inflation, not the 115× the amendment quotes for Letter HIGH. Amendment 7's **ratios**
(`ubs/lb` = 11.7× on Protein; I measure 11.86×) look more salvageable than amendment 11 implies. I
have not reopened it and the report does not rely on it.

### 4.2 "15.5 % of Letter LOW" has GED = 0

Quoted in the brief as motivation for keeping zeros. What I measure on `iam_letter_low` is
**10.53 %** of pairs with `UB == 0` (73,268 / 695,610) and **28.05 %** certified. The 15.5 % is
from `T-05-design.md` §1's *Reads that fail silently*, which attributes it to **Suite 1**'s exact
GED — a different cohort and a different quantity. The argument for keeping zeros is unaffected;
the number should not be requoted against Suite 2.

### 4.3 The bin-13 "97.1 % Mutagenicity" share cannot arise from §8's strata

The brief already says to compute dominance from the data rather than quoting §1.1's figures, and
that is what the module does. Recording the consequence: with §8's seven strata the top stratum is
`>40` and, on the eight datasets present, it is 66.1 % `aids_iam`. There is **no stratum in this
report to which the 97.1 % figure attaches**, and it should not be carried into any §7 table even

### 4.5 ~~`metadata.n_certified = 0` where 251 pairs are certified~~ — RETRACTED

I reported this as a defect. **It is not one, and the retraction is mine.** The coordinator checked
all 26 landed files: every one records `n_certified = 0` **and** an all-False off-diagonal
`certified_mask`, so metadata and array agree perfectly — 0 disagreements across 26 files. The
251 I counted are correct as a *computation*, but I obtained them by comparing `LB/ged_matrix`
against `UB/ged_matrix`, which is not what the file's own mask holds **until cross-fill runs**.
`CONTRACTS` §4.2 specifies exactly this: the mask is derived by a separate cross-fill step over two
separate campaigns, not written by the campaign that produced each file. Running
`approx_ged_crossfill` on a copy of `linux.npz` takes `n_certified` 0 → 79 and
`certification_rate` → 0.020174 in all three roles, matching the 79 off-diagonal `True` entries in

checking the contract.**

**The useful note that replaces it**, now recorded as limitation 11 in `REPORT.md`: reading
`certified_mask` or `metadata.n_certified` from a role file **before** cross-fill silently yields a
certification rate of exactly zero, for every dataset, raising nothing. That is a live trap for any
future ticket analysing a partially-finalised tree. This module is immune by construction — it
derives `|LB − UB| <= 1e-9` from the two `ged_matrix` arrays — and that immunity is now stated
rather than incidental.
